`short-translate` takes one of your takes and gives it back in another
language, in your own voice. With HeyGen the lips follow the new language too.
Then [short-editing](/editing/short-editing) edits the translated take like any
other, with captions transcribed in the new language.

It translates the **take**, not the finished short, so the editor cuts and
times everything on the translated voice. It also cleans the take first:
stutters and retakes sent to HeyGen would be translated, lip-synced and billed.

## Invoke

```bash
/short-translate my latest take in English
/short-translate short-my-slug to Spanish
/short-translate <take> --to en --from fr
```

It always shows the duration and the cost first, and sends nothing until you
say yes.

## What it does

1. **Transcribes the take** with word timestamps. A teleprompter take written
   with `studio-script` already has its script next to it: the translation of
   each line is filled in for you.
2. **Builds the cues**: one per spoken sentence, with a natural translation
   (not word for word, names, numbers and product names kept).
3. **Cuts the stumbles**: repeated words, false starts, a sentence said twice
   (the last good one stays). Pauses are tightened, each cut has a short fade.
   You see the kept length and the dropped ranges. It also writes a subtitle
   file timed on the cleaned take.
4. **Estimates, then sends** after your yes. It runs 2 to 20 minutes in the
   background, and a job can be resumed if it gets interrupted, without being
   billed twice.
5. **Hands the result to the editor.** The translated short is named
   `<original slug>-<lang>` so both versions sit side by side. When the
   original short has a plan (scenes, B-roll, music), it is reused and retimed
   on the translated take instead of starting from scratch. On-screen texts and
   the CTA are rewritten in the new language.

## Providers and modes

| Provider | What you get | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| HeyGen `precision` (default) | voice and best lip-sync, side views, hands in front of the face | about 10 credits per minute |
| HeyGen `speed` | voice and lip-sync, face mostly front-on | about 5 credits per minute |
| HeyGen `audio` | your voice, original image, no lip-sync | about 4 credits per minute |
| ElevenLabs | your cloned voice, original image, no lip-sync | ElevenLabs credits |

Lip-sync only matters when your face is on screen for long stretches. A short
full of B-roll or with a small webcam circle looks fine without it, and costs
2 to 2.5 times less.

## What you need

- `HEYGEN_API_KEY` in `.short-editing/.env` for HeyGen (HeyGen, Settings, API),
  or `ELEVEN_KEY` for ElevenLabs (already needed by the kit).
- Without the HeyGen key, ElevenLabs works in the meantime.
- HeyGen uploads are capped at 32 MB: a copy is re-encoded to fit. Beyond about
  15 minutes, cut the take into parts first.
