`broll` turns your folder of footage into a library you can search in plain
words. It reads each file once (date, place, length, six frames), has fast and
almost free Haiku agents describe every clip, and from then on "me at my desk at
night" finds the right shot in one query, without anyone opening a file.

Then it places the clips in your short for you, after looking at each one.

## Invoke

```bash
/broll setup
/broll add ~/Movies/lisbon-trip
/broll add --source ~/Pictures/Screens
/broll use
/broll search "sunset beach" --portrait
/broll reject b3f2a1c9d "ugly framing"
/broll stats
```

You can also just talk: "add these videos", "put my B-roll on this short",
"this clip is bad, drop it", "find me a sunset".

## What it does

1. **`setup`, once.** It asks where your clips are, scans them (about one second
   per clip), reads the date and GPS stored in each file, names the places, and
   extracts six frames per clip. Nothing is guessed: no GPS, no place.
2. **Tagging by `broll-tagger` agents.** Each agent reads one page of 5 clips
   (6 frames each) and writes a title, a description, the mood, the emotions,
   keywords, and whether you are on screen. Pages run in parallel, about 20
   seconds per page. A big library gets its first 100 clips now and the rest
   later.
3. **Finding you on screen.** Give it one to three close-up photos of your face
   so the taggers can tell which clips show you.
4. **A check with you.** It runs three searches on your real topics and shows
   the contact sheet ("here is what I find for « desk at night »"). A wrong tag
   is fixed on the spot.
5. **`add`** copies new files in (or `--move`), scans and tags only the new
   ones, and tells you what arrived ("12 clips added: 4 in Lisbon, 8 at the
   office").
6. **`use`** reads the words of the short in progress, picks the lines that show
   a moment, a place, a feeling or you doing something, runs one search per
   line, and **looks at every candidate** before placing it. A search score is
   never a validation. The plan is saved in `files/broll-plan.json`, then
   written into the short.
7. **After the render**, the clips you kept are marked as used so the next video
   prefers fresh ones.

Numbers, ideas and proof stay motion design. No good clip for a line means the
library does not have that image: you get motion design or a generated image,
never a random shot.

Full-screen B-roll lasts 2 to 5 seconds and starts on the word that names it.
A screen recording the viewer must read goes as a card on the paper instead.

## See the library

The B-roll tab of the studio shows every clip as a card (hover to scrub), a
search bar ranked like `search`, filters (me on screen, portrait, landscape,
never used, place, rejected), and a panel per clip to play it, copy its path,
mark it as "me" or reject it.

## What you need

- A folder of clips, anywhere: it stays where it is (`--source`) or is copied
  into your project's `B-roll` folder.
- The `broll-tagger` agent (installed with the kit) for the tagging.
- Your files are never deleted or moved unless you pass `--move`. A rejected
  clip is only hidden from searches and can be restored.
